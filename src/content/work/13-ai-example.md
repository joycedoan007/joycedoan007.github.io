## The challenge

New AI tools ship every week, and most lists are written for engineers or paid for by the tools they rank. A designer, marketer or founder who just needs to get a job done has to wade through jargon and sponsored hype to find out which tool fits. AI Radar is built for those non-technical creators: independent, plain-language and organised around the job, not the model.

<div class="overline" style="color: var(--text-accent); margin-bottom: 8px;">Approach</div>

## How I designed it

The product is organised around the questions a newcomer actually asks, which became six tabs: **Find AI Tools**, **How to Use AI**, **Best Tools by Task**, **Tool Combinations**, **Compare Tools** and **Get Advice**.

The directory leads with a single headline, "Find the right AI tool for you", and then narrows quickly. Nine category filters cover marketing, 2D, 3D, video, motion, UI/UX, coding and audio and writing. Three sort modes (most capable, fastest, most affordable) let people rank by what they care about. Each of the 36 cards pairs a short badge such as "Best For Beginners" with a plain-language description, so a tool can be judged at a glance before opening its details.

Comparison is built into the browsing flow instead of living on a separate page: a Compare button on every card stages tools in an Arena that holds up to three, which keeps side-by-side decisions manageable. A rotating "Latest AI news" ticker keeps the directory feeling current, and the whole interface switches between English and Vietnamese.

<div class="overline" style="color: var(--text-accent); margin-bottom: 8px;">Outcome</div>

AI Radar is live as a working product:
